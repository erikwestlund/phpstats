phit aims to give the same statistical results as R and lme4 for the same data and model. This page lists what phit reproduces, where its results or conventions differ, and what it does not support. Agreement is established on the tested cases listed under Evidence and tolerances, not for every possible input. Full R/lme4 compatibility is a goal, not a claim about the current release.

Base R’s stats package supplies lm() and glm(). lme4 is a separate R package.

Differences from R

These are the differences in results and inference. Model formulas lists the differences in formula handling.

  • GLM covariance. phit reports the inverse information at the final coefficients. See GLM covariance.
  • glmer Laplace modes. phit solves each conditional mode to convergence. lme4’s default tolPwrss stops earlier. See glmer conditional modes.
  • Mixed-model inference. phit reports Wald z tests and p-values for fixed effects. lme4’s Gaussian summary reports t values without p-values. phit has no degrees-of-freedom correction (Satterthwaite or Kenward–Roger).
  • Group and term order. Groups follow first appearance after deletion, and random terms keep formula order. lme4 sorts levels and orders terms by their number of levels. Estimates do not depend on the order.
  • Rank deficiency. An aliased fixed column raises code 1004. R reports NA coefficients.
  • Zero weights and zero-column designs are rejected. R accepts them.
  • Nonconvergence raises an exception. R can return a fit object with a warning.
  • Sparse versus dense computation. lme4 uses a sparse Cholesky factor for every design. phit splits single and nested grouping terms into independent blocks, but crossed terms use a dense summary limited to 512 random coefficients and 50,000,000 entries in the initial augmented matrix.
  • Random numbers. Seeded sampling uses local ChaCha20 state, so sequences differ from R’s generators.
  • Vector semantics. Distribution and descriptive functions do not implement R’s recycling, coercion, or vector names.

GLM covariance

GLM covariances in phit are the inverse Fisher information at the final coefficients. R’s vcov() and summary() for a glm use the working weights of the last IRLS iteration, which come from the coefficients one step before the final update. R’s coefficients are already at the maximum to many digits, but its covariance differs from the information at those coefficients by about the size of the last step. The difference does not come from the convergence tolerance, from Fisher versus observed information (equal for the canonical logit and log links), or from dispersion (fixed at one).

For am ~ wt + hp on mtcars:

  • The coefficients agree to 12 digits. R’s last step changed them by 3.4e-8 relative.
  • The variance of the wt coefficient is 9.41676694948 from vcov() and 9.41676794579 from phit, 1.1e-7 relative.
  • Recomputing the information in R at coef(fit), or refitting with start = coef(fit), gives 9.41676794579.

Near separation the last step is larger, and the two can differ by about 1e-4 relative. phit’s R fixtures restart glm() from its own solution until the coefficients stop changing.

glmer conditional modes

fit_mixed_logistic() evaluates the Laplace approximation with each group’s conditional mode solved to convergence. lme4’s glmer() finds the modes by penalized iteratively reweighted least squares (PIRLS) and stops at glmerControl(tolPwrss = 1e-7) by default, which perturbs its objective enough to move the estimates. For cbind(incidence, size - incidence) ~ period + (1 | herd) on lme4’s cbpp data, with BOBYQA at rhoend = 1e-12:

  • phit’s lme4 fixtures therefore fit glmer() with tolPwrss = 1e-12.

Not supported

Each item below is outside the current parity claim.

Formulas

  • subset and na.exclude arguments. Rows are deleted listwise, as na.omit does; see Missing values.
  • Contrasts other than treatment and contr.poly, and a contrasts argument.
  • Functions outside the supported transforms, such as ns(), bs(), scale(), and C(). They raise code 1007.
  • Multivariate lm responses, such as cbind(y1, y2) ~ x.

Fixed-effect models

  • GLM families and links other than Gaussian identity, binomial logit and probit, and Poisson log.
  • Fractional binomial weights.
  • Configurable confidence levels for coefficient intervals, profile and bootstrap intervals, and likelihood-ratio tests.
  • Residual diagnostics, leverage, and influence measures.
  • Model-object methods such as update(), anova(), and simulate().

Mixed models

  • Binomial random slopes or several random terms, and mixed Poisson, probit, or negative-binomial likelihoods. fit_mixed_logistic() supports one random intercept.
  • nlmer() and other nonlinear mixed models.
  • Sparse computation for crossed designs beyond the dense limits.
  • Native NLopt and PORT (nlminb) backends, the full lme4 optimizer controls, restart policies, gradient and Hessian convergence checks, and an allFit()-style audit.
  • Prediction standard errors and intervals, and simulation of new observations.
  • Fixed-effect degrees-of-freedom corrections, and intervals for variance components or random effects.

predict() covers point predictions, standard errors, and lm intervals for fixed-effect models, as described with the formulas. Mixed fits report the conditional variances of the random effects.

The R fixtures record each wrapper’s controls and warnings:

  • They use tightened optimizer controls and boundary.tol = 1e-3. At the default boundary tolerance, nloptwrap left one singular REML case just above zero and classified it as nonsingular. Tightening nlminb too far produced singular-convergence warnings.
  • Every saved reference run has convergence code zero and no warnings.
  • The multi-level BOBYQA references restart R from its own first fit with rhobeg = 0.001 and rhoend = 1e-9. No phit estimate seeds an R fit.

These fixtures validate explicit controls, not agreement under every package default.
