phit’s fit_probit_matrix(), R glm(binomial(link = "probit")), and statsmodels GLM with the probit link fit identical simulated observations at four sizes. The raw report keeps every timing sample, machine and runtime versions, input hashes, numerical outputs, and differences from R.

Timing boundaries

Each timed fit materializes coefficients, covariance, standard errors, normal z tests, p-values, Wald intervals, every fitted probability and response residual, likelihood, deviance, null deviance, AIC, and residual df.

Simulation and agreement

Study hours are uniform between 1 and 12. Practice scores are 45 + 3 × hours plus normal noise with standard deviation 8, clipped. The wider case adds independent standard-normal extra scores. Outcomes are draws from

\[\pi_i=\Phi(-1.9+0.13\,\mathrm{hours}_i+0.011\,\mathrm{score}_i +0.08\sum_j\mathrm{extra}_{ij}),\qquad y_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\pi_i).\]

This simulation is separate from the small teaching sample. Data hashes, generating coefficients, and sample correlations are recorded.

Coefficient-level quantities and scalar statistics are compared with R at 1e-9 + 1e-7 * abs(reference). Fitted values and residuals are computed in full and compared at ten evenly spaced rows; the small fixtures compare full vectors. phit must report convergence within 1 to 100 updates. Wald intervals follow R confint.default(), and the null baseline follows the documented intercept convention. Probit covariance uses expected Fisher information in all three runtimes.
